# Paid Ads

## When to use
Use to plan a paid campaign, write ad copy and creative briefs, structure accounts, analyse performance exports, or decide what to change next. Do not use for organic posting (see `social-media`).

**Money rule:** never create, enable, raise budgets on, or publish a live campaign, and never connect to an ad account with write access, without the user's explicit approval for that specific action and amount. Drafts and recommendations are fine.

## Gather first
- **Business economics**: price, gross margin, and either customer lifetime value or first-order value. From these, derive the most you can afford to pay per customer (target CPA) — ask, don't assume.
- **Conversion event**: what counts as success (purchase, qualified lead, trial start) and whether it is tracked reliably. If not, fix tracking first (`analytics-tracking`); optimising toward a broken signal wastes money.
- **Audience**: who buys, what they search for or care about, what objections they have, and what words they use (reviews, support tickets, sales call notes are good sources).
- **Budget and timeframe**, and any existing performance data (exports, screenshots).
- **Landing page**: read it. The ad's promise must match what the page delivers.

## Procedure
1. **Pick channels by intent.** People already searching for the solution → search ads. People who have the problem but are not searching → social/video, where creative does the targeting. B2B by job title → professional networks (usually costlier per click). Start with one or two channels, not five.
2. **Size the test.** A campaign needs enough conversions to learn from. Estimate expected cost per conversion and check the budget can buy a meaningful number in the test window; if it cannot, pick a cheaper, earlier conversion event or narrow the scope. Present this calculation to the user with assumptions labelled.
3. **Structure simply.** Few campaigns, separated by objective or funnel stage; within each, group by theme or audience. Use a consistent naming scheme (channel_objective_audience_creative_date) so reports are readable. Over-fragmentation starves each group of data.
4. **Targeting.** For search: tight keyword themes, match types appropriate to budget, and a negative keyword list from day one. For social: broad audiences with strong creative often outperform narrow interest stacks on modern platforms, but exclude existing customers and recent converters. Retargeting is a separate, smaller campaign.
5. **Write creative variations.** For each audience, produce several distinct *angles* (different reasons to care), not cosmetic rewrites:
   - Pain → relief
   - Outcome / transformation
   - Proof (specific, true result or customer quote the user can verify)
   - Objection handling ("no setup", "cancel anytime" — only if true)
   - Comparison to the status quo
   For each angle write: headline(s) within the platform's character limits (look them up; they change), primary text, CTA, and a visual brief (what the image or first three seconds of video shows). Check every claim against what the user can substantiate; ad platforms and consumer law penalise misleading claims.
6. **Launch checklist (prepare; user executes or approves):** conversion tracking fires on a test conversion, UTM parameters on every URL, budget caps set at campaign level, schedule and geography correct, policy-sensitive categories (health, finance, housing, employment, politics) reviewed.
7. **Optimise in order.** Let campaigns exit the learning phase before judging. Then: kill clear losers on cost per conversion (not CTR), shift budget to winners gradually, refresh creative when frequency rises and performance decays, add negatives from search term reports, improve the landing page when click-through is fine but conversion is not. Change one major thing at a time so you can attribute the effect.

## Pitfalls
- Judging on CTR or CPC instead of cost per real conversion and downstream quality.
- Trusting platform-reported conversions blindly; attribution windows and modelled conversions inflate them. Reconcile against backend sales.
- Scaling budget in big jumps, which can reset learning.
- Sending all traffic to the homepage.
- Fabricated urgency, fake testimonials, or before/after claims that cannot be backed.

## Deliver
Lead with the recommendation: channels, budget split, target CPA and the expected learning timeline (as an estimate). Then the account structure, creative variations in a table (angle, headline, body, CTA, visual brief), the tracking/launch checklist, and the optimisation rules. When analysing data, show the numbers you computed and from which file; say what you could not verify (e.g. platform attribution vs. actual revenue). End with the exact actions that need the user's approval before any money moves.
