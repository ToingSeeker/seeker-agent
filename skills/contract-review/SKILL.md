# Contract Review

Contract review answers: what does this document actually commit us to, which parts are worse than we can accept, and what wording would fix them.

**This is not legal advice and does not create a lawyer-client relationship.** It is a structured first read to help the user understand a document and prepare questions. A qualified lawyer in the relevant jurisdiction should review before signing anything significant, and must be involved in the cases listed under "Escalate". Never sign, accept, send redlines to the counterparty, or e-sign on the user's behalf without their explicit approval.

## When to use (and not)

Use for commercial agreements: NDAs, service agreements, SaaS terms, supplier/vendor contracts, freelancer and agency agreements, partnership letters, order forms. Do not use as the only review for: employment termination or settlement, real estate, financing and investment documents, M&A, litigation documents, or anything where the user is facing a deadline with serious consequences.

## What to gather first

- The full document, including referenced schedules, order forms, and linked terms. Note anything incorporated by reference that you cannot see.
- Which party the user is, and the deal context: what is being bought/sold, value, duration, what matters most.
- The user's position or playbook: limits they will accept (liability caps, payment terms, governing law). If none, ask two or three key questions or state the reasonable defaults you will assume.
- Governing law and jurisdiction, as written.

## Procedure

1. **Read the whole document first.** Definitions change the meaning of everything else; read them carefully, and note defined terms that are broader or narrower than they appear.
2. **Summarise the deal** in plain language: parties, what each must do, price and payment, term and renewal, how it ends.
3. **Walk the key clause areas** and for each record what the document says, whether it is acceptable against the user's position, and why:
   - Scope and deliverables; acceptance criteria.
   - Payment, price changes, late fees, taxes.
   - Term, auto-renewal, notice periods, termination rights (for cause and convenience) and what survives.
   - Liability: caps, exclusions, what is uncapped.
   - Indemnities: who covers what, and whether they are mutual.
   - Warranties and disclaimers.
   - Intellectual property ownership, licences granted, background IP, feedback clauses.
   - Confidentiality and data protection; personal data processing; security obligations.
   - Non-compete, non-solicit, exclusivity.
   - Assignment and change of control.
   - Governing law, dispute resolution, venue.
   - Unusual items: unilateral amendment rights, audit rights, most-favoured terms, liquidated damages.
4. **Rate each issue**: high (could cause serious loss or is unacceptable to the user), medium (should negotiate), low (note only).
5. **Propose redlines** for high and medium items: the exact replacement wording, a one-line rationale the user can give the other side, and a fallback position if the first ask is refused.
6. **Check what is missing**: clauses the user would expect that are absent (e.g. no cap at all, no termination for convenience, no data protection terms when personal data is involved).

## NDA triage

NDAs are high-volume; aim for a quick, reliable call:

- Mutual or one-way, and does that fit the situation?
- Definition of confidential information: reasonable, or covers everything forever?
- Standard exclusions present (already known, independently developed, publicly available, received from a third party lawfully).
- Duration of the obligation and of the agreement.
- Permitted disclosure to advisers and as required by law.
- Return/destruction terms that are practical.
- Hidden extras: non-solicit, non-compete, IP assignment, exclusivity, residuals clause, one-sided remedies.

Outcome: **sign as is**, **sign with small edits** (list them), or **needs real review** (say why).

## Escalate to a lawyer when

- Large value, long term, or exclusivity is involved.
- Uncapped liability, broad indemnities, or IP assignment of core assets.
- Personal data, regulated industries, cross-border issues, or unfamiliar governing law.
- The counterparty refuses reasonable changes on high-risk items.
- Anything involves an existing dispute.

## Pitfalls

- Reviewing a clause in isolation when a definition or another clause changes it.
- Stating what the law "requires" in a jurisdiction you have not checked; mark such points as questions for counsel.
- Redlines that read as aggressive when a small change would do.
- Missing the auto-renewal notice window; put any deadline dates in the summary.

## Deliverable

Lead with a verdict (sign / negotiate / get counsel) and the top three issues. Then: deal summary, issues table (clause reference, issue, severity, proposed redline, fallback), missing clauses, key dates. End with what you could not see (referenced documents), assumptions about the user's position, and the not-legal-advice note.
