# Content Strategy

## When to use
- A team wants to start or reset a blog, docs, resource hub, or content marketing effort.
- Existing content is not producing traffic, leads, or activation and they want a plan.
- They need keyword clustering or briefs for writers.

Not for: writing a single page (copywriting) or fixing technical SEO (seo-audit).

## Gather first
Ask only for what you cannot infer from the site and repo:
- The business goal content serves: awareness, signups, sales enablement, retention, support deflection.
- Who the audience is, what they are trying to get done, and what they already know.
- What the product does better than alternatives, and proof that exists (customers, data, expertise).
- Existing content inventory and its performance, if available.
- Capacity: who writes, how often, and with what subject expertise.

## Procedure

### 1. Map audience problems
For each audience, list the problems and questions they have at each stage: noticing the problem,
comparing solutions, choosing, using the product, getting more value. Sources: support tickets, sales
call notes, community threads, reviews of competitors, search autocomplete, the product's own docs.
These questions are the raw material; keywords come later.

### 2. Define pillars
Pick a small number of themes where three things overlap: the audience cares, the product is relevant,
and the team has real expertise or data. Content outside these pillars needs a strong reason.

### 3. Build topic clusters
- Under each pillar, gather candidate queries and questions.
- Cluster by **intent**, not just shared words: queries that the same page would fully satisfy belong
  together. A practical check: if the top search results for two queries largely overlap, they likely
  share a page. Where you cannot check results, group by what the searcher wants to do and note that the
  clustering is judgement-based.
- Each cluster becomes one page with a primary topic and supporting subtopics. Choose a pillar/hub page
  per theme and link the cluster pages to it.
- Map clusters to existing pages to find gaps, overlaps (cannibalisation), and pages to update rather
  than rewrite.

### 4. Choose formats
Match format to intent: how-to guide, comparison, template or tool, original research, case study,
glossary entry, video walkthrough, docs page. Prefer formats the team can make better than what already
ranks. Original data and real examples are the hardest to copy.

### 5. Prioritise
Score each idea against business value, audience demand, ability to win (competition and the team's
authority), and effort. Use whatever data exists; if search volumes are unavailable, say so and rank by
qualitative signals. Quick wins are often updates to pages that already get impressions.

### 6. Write briefs
For each prioritised piece, a brief covers: working title, target reader and their situation, the
question it answers, search intent, primary and secondary topics, the outline, unique angle or proof to
include, internal links in and out, call to action, and what "done" means. Keep briefs tight enough to
fit on one screen.

### 7. Calendar and distribution
Sequence pieces so pillar pages ship before the cluster pages that link to them. Plan distribution for
each piece (newsletter, social, community, sales team, in-product) rather than assuming search will
find it. Build in time to refresh old content.

### 8. Measurement
Pick metrics per goal: impressions and clicks for search reach, signups or assisted conversions for
acquisition, activation for onboarding content, ticket volume for support content. Set a review cadence
and decide in advance what result would cause a piece to be updated, merged, or dropped.

## Pitfalls
- Choosing topics by volume alone, producing traffic that never converts.
- One article per keyword variant, creating pages that compete with each other.
- Generic content any competitor or language model could produce. The plan should state each piece's
  unique contribution.
- Plans bigger than the team can sustain. A smaller cadence kept is better than a big one abandoned.
- Presenting invented search volumes or difficulty scores. Label estimates.

## Deliver
Lead with the pillars and the first five pieces to produce, with a one-line reason each. Then: cluster
map (pillar, cluster, primary topic, intent, existing URL or gap), prioritised backlog, two or three
example briefs, a calendar for the first period, and the measurement plan. Say which inputs came from
real data and which from judgement.
