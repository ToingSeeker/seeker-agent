# Lead Magnets and Free Tools

## When to use
- Growing an email list or pipeline with something of value in exchange.
- Earning links and search traffic with a useful free tool.
- Turning existing expertise or product logic into a standalone asset.

When not to use: the audience is undefined (fix that first), or there is no follow-up path - an email list nobody nurtures is wasted effort.

## Gather first
- Who should this attract, and what do they do next if it works (trial, demo, purchase)?
- The product's core job and what the audience struggles with just before they need it.
- Existing assets: data, internal spreadsheets, scripts, frameworks, past content that performed.
- Distribution: list size, social reach, partners, search demand.
- Build capacity and where it will be hosted.

## Choosing the idea
Score candidates on:
- **Adjacency**: solves a problem one step before the product's job, so users self-qualify.
- **Specificity**: one clear outcome ("estimate your monthly X") beats a broad ebook.
- **Speed to value**: useful in minutes.
- **Search or share demand**: people already look for it, or would send it to a colleague.
- **Build cost and maintenance**: tools that depend on data that changes need an owner.
- **Defensibility**: uses something we know or have that others do not.

Common formats: calculators and estimators, graders or checkers that analyse an input, templates and checklists, generators, benchmark reports from aggregated data, short courses, swipe files. Free tools usually earn more links and repeat use than PDFs; PDFs are cheaper to make.

## Procedure
1. **Validate demand cheaply.** Search for the problem phrase, check whether similar tools exist and how good they are, and look at community threads asking for it. If a good free one exists, find a sharper angle or skip it.
2. **Define the output.** Write what the user gets in one sentence and sketch the result screen or document first. The result is the product.
3. **Decide the gate.**
   - Ungated: maximum reach, links and shares; capture email with an optional "send me the results" or save feature.
   - Partially gated: show a useful result, gate the detailed report or download.
   - Fully gated: highest capture rate per visitor, lowest reach. Fits high-intent, high-value assets.
   Ask for the minimum fields; each extra field costs completions.
4. **Build.** For tools: keep logic transparent (show assumptions and formulas), handle bad input gracefully, make it fast on mobile, and make results shareable by URL where sensible. For documents: practical over long; every page should help someone act. Run the tool against several realistic inputs and confirm the outputs are correct before launch.
5. **Connect the follow-up.** Deliver the asset instantly, then a short sequence that helps them use it and introduces the product where it naturally fits. Respect consent and local email/privacy rules; make opt-in explicit.
6. **Make it findable.** A dedicated landing page targeting the search phrase, internal links from relevant pages, an embed or share option, outreach to people who have written about the problem.
7. **Instrument.** Track views, starts, completions, captures, downstream activation or pipeline, and links earned. Fire events at each step so drop-off is visible.
8. **Iterate.** Improve the step with the biggest drop. Refresh data-driven assets on a schedule.

## Pitfalls
- Building something impressive that nobody searches for or shares.
- Attracting the wrong audience (a generic giveaway grows the list, not revenue).
- Gating so hard that nobody links to it.
- Tool outputs that are wrong or unexplained - this damages trust in the main product.
- No owner, so the tool breaks quietly.

## Deliver
Lead with the recommended idea and why it beat the alternatives (show the scoring briefly). Then: the spec (inputs, outputs, gate, fields), the build or content, the landing page copy, the follow-up sequence outline, and the tracking plan. State what was tested (inputs tried, outputs checked) and what was not. Ask before publishing, sending emails, or connecting to the user's email provider.
