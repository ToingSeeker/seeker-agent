# Deal and Pipeline Review

The purpose of a deal review is to replace optimism with evidence. A deal is at the stage its evidence supports, not the stage someone typed into a CRM.

Use this for one opportunity (deep review and close plan) or a list of them (pipeline triage and forecast). For preparing a single upcoming call, use `sales-call-prep`.

## What to gather first

- The deal record(s): stage, amount, close date, owner, last activity. A CRM export (CSV) is ideal; read it rather than asking the user to summarise.
- Notes, call summaries, and email threads for any deal being reviewed in depth.
- The team's stage definitions, if they exist. If not, use the evidence-based stages below and say so.
- The period being forecast and any quota or target, if the user wants a gap analysis.

## Evidence-based stages

Define each stage by an observable fact on the buyer side. A reasonable default:

1. **Qualified**: a real problem confirmed by someone who owns it.
2. **Solution fit**: the buyer agrees our approach addresses that problem; key requirements known.
3. **Decision mapped**: we know who signs, who can block, the approval steps, and the budget source.
4. **Proposal accepted in principle**: pricing and scope shared and not rejected; mutual timeline agreed.
5. **Commit**: paperwork in legal/procurement with a known path to signature.

If a deal's evidence matches an earlier stage than its recorded one, flag the mismatch.

## Single-deal procedure

1. Reconstruct the timeline from the notes: first contact, key meetings, what changed, last buyer-initiated action. Buyer-initiated activity matters more than seller activity.
2. Check each qualification area and mark it **confirmed**, **assumed**, or **unknown**: problem and its cost, decision maker, decision process, budget, timing driver (why now), competition and status quo, technical fit, legal/procurement path.
3. List risks, each with a concrete signal: single-threaded (one contact only), no compelling event, close date moved more than once, champion without authority, procurement not started near quarter end, silence since the proposal, discounts requested before value was agreed.
4. List gaps as questions the seller must answer, and who can answer them.
5. Build the close plan: work backwards from the target signature date through every step the buyer must take (technical validation, security review, legal redlines, budget approval, signature). Each step gets an owner on each side and a date. If the backward plan does not fit before the close date, say the date is not realistic and propose one that is.
6. Give a verdict: stage the evidence supports, forecast category, and the single most important action this week.

## Pipeline procedure

1. Load the data and check its quality first: missing amounts, close dates in the past, deals with no activity in weeks, duplicates. Report these before any analysis; bad data produces confident nonsense.
2. Segment by stage and close period. Compute totals by forecast category and coverage against target if one was given. Show the arithmetic.
3. Flag deals for discussion: largest, oldest in stage, slipped close dates, stage-evidence mismatches, and anything closing this period without a mapped decision process.
4. Spot patterns: where deals stall, sources that convert poorly, reps whose close dates slip repeatedly. Present patterns as observations to investigate, not verdicts on people.

## Honest forecasting

- Use explicit categories: **commit** (would be surprised if it slipped), **best case** (could close if listed things go right), **pipeline** (not this period realistically), **omitted**.
- A deal belongs in commit only with a mapped path to signature and a confirmed timing driver.
- If historical win rates by stage are available, apply them and show the calculation. If not, do not invent conversion rates; give the category totals and say a weighted number would need history.
- Report a range, not a single heroic figure, and list the deals that swing it.

## Pitfalls

- Do not accept "they love it" as evidence. Ask what the buyer did, not what they said.
- Do not average away risk; one large uncertain deal can dominate the number.
- Do not change CRM data or email anyone. Recommend changes; the user applies them.

## Deliverable

Lead with the verdict: for one deal, real stage + forecast category + top action; for a pipeline, the forecast range and the three to five deals that decide it. Then the supporting detail: qualification grid, risks, close plan table, data-quality issues. Close with what was taken from records versus what you inferred, and what you could not check.
