# Sales Call Prep and Post-Call Summary

Two modes share one goal: every customer conversation should move a real buying decision forward by one concrete step.

- **Prep mode**: before a call. Output is a one-page brief the seller can read in five minutes.
- **Summary mode**: after a call. Output is a factual record plus a follow-up draft.

Do not use this for pipeline-wide forecasting or judging deal health across many opportunities; use `deal-review` for that.

## What to gather first

Ask only for what you cannot find yourself:

- Company name and website; the people attending (names, titles) if known.
- The call type: first discovery, demo, technical deep-dive, pricing, renewal, expansion.
- What we sell and the one or two products likely relevant.
- Prior history: earlier notes, emails, CRM export, or the last summary. Read any files the user points to.
- For summary mode: the transcript or raw notes. Never invent content that is not in them.

## Prep mode procedure

1. **Research the account from public sources.** Search the web and fetch the company site, recent news, press releases, job postings, and (if public) filings or investor material. Job postings often reveal priorities and tooling better than marketing copy. Note the date of every fact; anything older than about a year should be flagged as possibly stale.
2. **Profile the attendees.** Role, likely responsibilities, and what each person is probably measured on. Do not speculate about personal life. If you cannot find someone, say so rather than guessing.
3. **Write the situation summary.** Three to five sentences: what the company does, size and stage, anything that changed recently (funding, leadership change, expansion, layoffs, regulation), and how that might create a need.
4. **Form hypotheses, not conclusions.** Two to four statements like "Because they are opening new regions, they likely struggle with X." Each hypothesis must name the evidence behind it and how to test it on the call.
5. **Draft questions.** Aim for eight to twelve, grouped by purpose: current situation, pain and its cost, who decides and how, timing and triggers, what they have tried. Prefer open questions. Include one or two that could disqualify the deal early; finding out it is a poor fit is a good outcome.
6. **Anticipate objections.** List the most likely two or three and a short, honest response to each. Do not promise features or pricing the user has not confirmed.
7. **Define the desired next step.** One specific, calendar-able outcome (e.g. "technical session with their platform lead next week"), plus a fallback if the primary is not reachable.

## Summary mode procedure

1. Read the whole transcript or notes before writing anything.
2. Extract, quoting or closely reflecting what was actually said:
   - Their stated problems and any numbers they gave (cost, volume, deadlines).
   - Decision process: who else is involved, approval steps, budget status, timing.
   - Objections or concerns raised, and whether they were resolved.
   - Commitments made by each side, with owners and dates.
   - Competitors or alternatives mentioned.
3. Separate **heard** from **inferred**. Mark any interpretation clearly ("seems", "not confirmed").
4. List gaps: what we still need to learn to advance the deal.
5. Draft the follow-up email: thank them briefly, restate their priorities in their words, confirm agreed next steps with dates, attach or promise only what was actually promised. Keep it short enough to read on a phone.
6. If the user keeps a CRM, offer field-ready text (stage, next step, close date notes) but do not update any system yourself unless asked.

## Quality bar and pitfalls

- Every claim in the brief is traceable to a source or labelled as a hypothesis.
- No flattering filler about the prospect; no generic questions that would fit any company.
- Do not overstate certainty about budget or authority; "unknown" is a valid answer and a useful one.
- Avoid scraping behind logins or using personal data beyond professional public profiles.
- Never send the follow-up email or message anyone. Draft it and let the user send it.

## Deliverable

Prep mode, in this order:

1. Desired outcome of the call (one line)
2. Situation summary
3. Attendees and what each cares about
4. Hypotheses with evidence and how to test each
5. Question list, grouped
6. Likely objections and responses
7. Next step and fallback
8. Sources consulted, with dates; note anything you could not verify

Summary mode: key facts, commitments table (who / what / by when), open gaps, risks, then the follow-up draft. End by stating what came from the transcript versus what you inferred.
