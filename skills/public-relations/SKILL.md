# Public Relations

## When to use
- A launch, funding round, milestone, original data, or expert opinion worth coverage.
- Building credibility and backlinks through earned media and listings.
- Preparing a press kit before anyone asks for one.

When not to use: there is no news and no useful angle - forcing it burns journalist goodwill. Crisis communications, legal disputes, or regulated disclosures (for example securities announcements) need a qualified PR or legal professional; this skill is not legal advice.

## Gather first
- What is happening, when, and what can be said publicly (embargoes, confidential numbers).
- The audience whose attention matters, then the outlets they read.
- Proof: customer stories willing to go on record, data, founder background, visuals.
- Spokesperson availability.

## Find the angle
Journalists cover stories, not products. Test each angle:
- **New**: is something genuinely first, or changed?
- **Relevant**: does it connect to a trend, debate, or event the outlet already covers?
- **Human**: is there a person with a story behind it?
- **Evidence**: original data, a surprising result, a concrete outcome?
- **Local or niche**: trade and regional outlets welcome specific stories that national ones ignore.
Write each candidate angle as a headline the outlet might actually run. Discard any that only make sense to your own team.

## Procedure
1. **Press kit.** One page or folder with: a short company description, the announcement, founder bios and headshots, logos in light and dark versions, product screenshots, key facts (founded, location, team size only if comfortable sharing), customer quotes with permission, and a press contact. Host it on a stable public page.
2. **Release (optional).** A release is a reference document, not a pitch. Headline with the news, a first paragraph answering who, what, when, where, why, one or two real quotes that say something, supporting detail, boilerplate, contact. Keep claims verifiable.
3. **Build a target list.** Find journalists who covered similar stories recently by searching outlets and bylines. Record name, outlet, beat, a recent relevant article, and contact route. Aim for a focused list of people who fit, not a mass blast. Newsletters, podcasts and niche communities often matter more than big outlets.
4. **Write pitches.** Short, personal, plain text. Open with why this matters to their readers, reference their recent work genuinely, give the angle in two sentences, offer the asset (data, interview, exclusive, embargoed preview), and stop. One follow-up after several days is fine; more is not.
5. **Exclusives and embargoes.** Offering one outlet an exclusive can win better coverage than a wide release. Respect embargo terms and confirm them before sending details.
6. **Directories and listings.** For launches, compile a list of relevant product directories, launch communities, marketplaces, industry associations and review sites. For each, note audience fit, rules (self-promotion policies, timing, required assets), and whether links are followed. Prepare a reusable asset pack: names, tagline variants by length, descriptions, categories, screenshots, logo. Prioritise the few where your buyers actually browse; low-quality link directories are not worth it. Never post where rules prohibit it or use fake accounts or votes.
7. **Prepare for interest.** Brief the spokesperson with key messages, likely tough questions, and what not to disclose. Make sure the site and signup can handle a traffic spike.
8. **Track.** Log pitches, replies, coverage, referral traffic, signups, and links. Thank journalists who covered you; build relationships beyond one launch.

## Pitfalls
- Pitching features instead of a story.
- Generic mass emails with mail-merge errors.
- Claims you cannot back up; journalists check.
- Treating coverage as the goal rather than reaching the audience.
- Paying for "guaranteed coverage" schemes that publish on low-value sites.

## Deliver
Lead with the chosen angles and why. Then the press kit contents, release draft if wanted, target list, personalised pitch drafts, directory list with asset pack, and a timeline. Never send pitches, submit listings, or publish anything without the user's explicit approval; drafts only until then. Note any facts that still need confirmation.
