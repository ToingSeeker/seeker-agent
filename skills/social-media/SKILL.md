# Social Media

## When to use
Use for organic posts, captions, threads, short-video scripts, content calendars, and turning existing material (articles, talks, docs, release notes) into platform-native posts. Do not use for paid promotion (see `paid-ads`). Never publish, schedule or reply on a live account without explicit confirmation.

## Gather first
- **Goal**: awareness, traffic, signups, community, hiring, credibility. It changes what a "good" post is.
- **Account and audience**: who follows (or should), what they already respond to. If the user can share past posts with their numbers, read them — the best predictor of what works on an account is that account's own history.
- **Voice**: three or four adjectives plus words to avoid. Ask for a few posts they consider on-brand.
- **Raw material**: source content, product facts, customer stories they have permission to share.
- **Platforms and capacity**: how many posts per week the user can realistically sustain. Consistency beats bursts.

## Platform norms (check current rules; they shift)
- **LinkedIn**: professional context, first two lines decide whether "see more" is clicked; text posts, documents/carousels and personal experience tend to travel; links in the post body may reduce reach — test.
- **X / threads-style networks**: brevity, opinions, threads for depth; replies and quote-posts are part of distribution.
- **Instagram**: visual first; carousels for teaching, Reels for reach; caption supports the image; hashtags are for discovery, use a few relevant ones.
- **TikTok / short video**: first one to two seconds carry the hook; native, unpolished often beats produced; on-screen text and captions for sound-off viewing.
- **Facebook groups, Reddit, forums**: community rules first; self-promotion is often banned or tolerated only from active members. Read the rules before suggesting a post.
- **YouTube / long video**: title + thumbnail are the hook; descriptions with chapters help search.
Look up current character limits, aspect ratios and video length limits rather than relying on memory.

## Procedure
1. **Define 3–5 content themes** tied to the goal and to what the audience wants: teaching (how-to), proof (results, case stories), perspective (opinions, lessons), behind-the-scenes, and occasional direct offers. Most posts should give value; a minority should ask.
2. **Write the hook first.** The opening line or first seconds must earn attention honestly. Useful patterns: a specific surprising fact (true and sourced), a concrete promise of what the reader will learn, a tension or mistake, a short story opening in the middle of action, a contrarian take the account genuinely holds. Write several hooks per post and pick the strongest.
3. **Write the body native to the platform.** Short paragraphs or line breaks; one idea per post; concrete examples over abstractions; end with a natural next step (a question, a save-worthy summary, or a link where appropriate). Keep posts accessible: alt text for images, captions on video, emoji and hashtags never replacing words.
4. **Repurpose deliberately.** From one long piece, extract: the core argument (one post), each sub-point (a carousel or thread), a striking quote or number (image post), a story (short video script), a checklist (save-worthy post). Rewrite for each platform; never cross-post identical text everywhere.
5. **Build the calendar.** A table with date, platform, theme, format, hook, asset needed, status. Mix themes across the week. Leave room for reactive posts. Batch-create to make it sustainable.
6. **Plan engagement.** Replying to comments soon after posting and engaging with others' posts in the niche usually matters as much as posting. Note who on the team does this.
7. **Measure.** Pick metrics that match the goal: saves and shares (value), comments (conversation), profile visits and link clicks (interest), follower growth (slow signal), and downstream signups via UTM-tagged links. Review monthly: which themes and formats over-perform relative to the account's own average, then do more of those.

## Pitfalls
- Engagement bait ("comment YES"), fake scarcity, or invented statistics.
- Using trending audio, images or others' content without the rights.
- Posting customer names, screenshots with personal data, or testimonials without permission.
- Judging a format on one post; compare against several.
- Writing in a corporate voice on platforms that reward a human one.

## Deliver
Lead with the plan in one paragraph (goal, platforms, cadence, themes). Then the posts — each with platform, hook options, final copy, visual/video brief, alt text, hashtags if relevant — and the calendar table. Note what you assumed about platform limits and what the user must check (rights to assets, facts, permissions). Do not post or schedule anything without the user's go-ahead.
