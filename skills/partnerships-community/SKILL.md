# Partnerships and Community

## When to use
Use for three related jobs: (1) co-marketing with companies that share your audience, (2) paid or gifted collaborations with creators/influencers, (3) starting or running a community around a product or topic. Not for affiliate commission programs (see `referral-programs`) or cold sales (see `cold-outreach`). Any outreach, contract or public post needs the user's approval before it goes out. Contracts and disclosure rules are legal matters — this is not legal advice; have a qualified person review agreements.

## Gather first
- **Audience**: who you want to reach and where they already spend attention.
- **What you bring**: audience size and engagement, product, data, expertise, budget, distribution channels. Partnerships are trades; know your side.
- **Goal**: signups, leads, awareness in a new segment, integrations used, community members who stay.
- **Capacity**: who will do the work. Partnerships and communities fail more often from neglect than from bad ideas.

## Co-marketing
1. **Find partners** with high audience overlap but no direct competition: tools used alongside yours, service providers to the same buyers, complementary creators or publications. Sources: your integrations list, what customers use alongside you (ask them), conference sponsor lists, newsletters in the niche.
2. **Assess fit** on audience overlap, audience size relative to yours, brand compatibility, engagement quality, and how easy they are to work with. Prefer partners of similar size for balanced deals.
3. **Propose a concrete idea**, not "let's partner": a joint guide or report, a webinar, an integration launch, a bundle or exclusive offer, a newsletter swap, a joint event. Show what's in it for them and what you'll do.
4. **Agree in writing**: deliverables per side, dates, who owns leads and how they're shared (and under what consent — sharing personal data between companies has privacy-law implications), approval rights on copy and logos, and how results are reported.
5. **Execute and report** with shared UTM-tagged links so each side can see impact. A good first campaign often leads to a second.

## Creator and influencer deals
1. **Choose creators by audience fit and trust**, not follower count. Look at comment quality, consistency, and whether the audience matches your buyer. Smaller niche creators often outperform larger generic ones for specific products — test.
2. **Check for red flags**: sudden follower spikes, engagement that doesn't match reach, generic comments, past controversies, competitor deals.
3. **Brief, don't script.** Give the key message, must-include facts, claims that are not allowed, the CTA and link/code, and let them speak in their own voice.
4. **Disclosure is mandatory.** Paid, gifted or affiliate relationships must be clearly disclosed on the content in most jurisdictions (e.g. FTC guidance in the US, advertising standards elsewhere). Use platform disclosure tools plus clear wording. Put this in the agreement.
5. **Terms to settle**: fee or gifting, deliverables and formats, timing, approval process, usage rights (can you reuse it in ads? for how long?), exclusivity, payment terms, and what happens if they don't deliver.
6. **Measure** with unique links/codes per creator: clicks, conversions, cost per acquisition, and content quality for reuse.

## Community
1. **Decide the purpose** in one sentence: what members get that they can't get elsewhere (help, peers, early access, learning, status). A product support forum and a practitioner community are different things.
2. **Pick the platform** where members already are and that fits the interaction: forum (searchable, durable), chat (fast, ephemeral), or social groups. Consider ownership and data portability.
3. **Seed before opening.** Invite a small group of engaged users, set norms, and have real conversations in place so newcomers don't walk into an empty room.
4. **Write a short code of conduct** and moderation plan: what is not allowed, how reports are handled, who moderates and when. Enforce it consistently.
5. **Create rhythms**: welcome threads, weekly prompts, office hours, member showcases, events. Spotlight members, not just the company.
6. **Measure health**: active members (not total), percentage of posts answered, member-to-member answers, retention of new members, and contributions back to the product (feedback, content, referrals).

## Pitfalls
- One-sided pitches that only benefit you.
- Undisclosed sponsorships.
- Paying for reach with fake or mismatched audiences.
- Launching a community and leaving it to members with no moderation.
- Treating community as a broadcast channel.

## Deliver
Lead with the recommendation: which of the three levers to pull first and why. Then the partner/creator shortlist with fit notes and sources (verified vs. assumed), pitch drafts, deal term checklist, community plan, and metrics. Never send pitches, sign up for platforms, or post publicly without the user's explicit go-ahead.
