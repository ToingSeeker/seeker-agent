# Marketing Plan

## When to use
- Launching a product or entering a new segment.
- Planning a quarter or year of marketing.
- "We need more customers, what should we do?" with no plan in place.

When not to use: the ask is a single tactic (one email, one ad) - handle it directly. If positioning or the audience is unknown, do customer research or build the product context document first; a plan on a vague audience is guesswork.

## Gather first
- Product context: what it is, who it is for, price, proof. Read any existing product context document before asking.
- Business goal behind the plan (revenue, signups, pipeline, retention) and the time frame.
- Current numbers: traffic, conversion rates, acquisition sources, CAC and LTV if known, sales cycle.
- Resources: budget, people, skills, existing assets (audience, list, content, partners).
- Constraints: regulated claims, brand rules, regions, seasonality.
Ask only for what cannot be found in the repo, docs or analytics.

## Procedure
1. **Situation.** Summarise where things stand: what is working, what is not, the market and competitor context, and the main constraint on growth (awareness, conversion, activation, retention, or capacity). Name the constraint; the plan should attack it.
2. **Audience.** Define one primary segment specifically enough to find them: role or situation, trigger event, where they already spend attention, what they use today. Add secondary segments only if resources allow.
3. **Positioning.** One sentence: for whom, which problem, what we are instead of the alternative, and why we are believable. Note the key messages and proof points per segment.
4. **Goals.** One north-star outcome and a few supporting metrics, each with a baseline, target and date. Work backwards through the funnel to show the targets are plausible (visitors needed x conversion = signups). If they are not plausible, say so.
5. **Channels.** List candidate channels, then score each roughly on reach to this audience, cost, time to results, and fit with the team's skills. Pick a small number - typically one or two core bets plus a cheap experiment or two. Explain why the rest were deprioritised.
6. **Ideas.** For each chosen channel, generate tactics broadly before narrowing. Useful prompts:
   - What does the audience already search for, read, attend, or ask peers about?
   - What can we give away that is genuinely useful (tools, templates, data, education)?
   - Which partners already have this audience?
   - What does the product itself produce that is shareable or referable?
   - What would be remarkable enough for someone to mention unprompted?
7. **Psychology lenses.** Review messaging and flows against well-established behavioural ideas, used honestly:
   - Reduce friction and effort at each step; defaults matter.
   - Social proof that is real and specific to the reader's situation.
   - Loss framing versus gain framing - test both.
   - Anchoring in pricing presentation.
   - Commitment through small first steps.
   - Clarity over cleverness; cognitive load kills conversion.
   Do not use fake scarcity, fake urgency, hidden costs, or confirm-shaming. They erode trust and can break consumer protection law.
8. **Budget.** Allocate by channel and phase, with a reserve for doubling down on what works. Show the cost assumption behind each line.
9. **Calendar.** A week-by-week or month-by-month plan with owners, dependencies, and launch dates. Front-load measurement setup.
10. **Measurement.** For each tactic: the metric, how it is tracked, the check-in cadence, and the kill or scale criteria. Decide in advance when an experiment has failed.
11. **Risks.** What could derail it and the fallback.

## Quality bar
- Every tactic traces back to the audience, the constraint and a goal.
- Numbers have stated assumptions; nothing invented is presented as benchmark data. Label rules of thumb as such.
- Focus beats coverage: a plan with twelve channels and two people will fail.
- A reader should know what happens on Monday.

## Deliver
A plan document in this order: summary (the bet, the goal, the budget), situation, audience, positioning, goals, channel choices with reasoning, tactics, budget, calendar, measurement, risks, open questions. Flag every assumption that needs the user's confirmation. Never launch campaigns, spend money, or publish anything without explicit approval.
