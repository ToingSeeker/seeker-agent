# Performance Reviews

A useful review tells someone, with evidence, what they achieved, how they worked, and what to do next. Fairness comes from judging everyone against the same expectations with real examples, not from soft wording.

**This is not HR or legal advice.** Reviews can affect pay, promotion, and employment. If a review involves potential termination, a performance improvement plan, a disability or leave situation, a complaint of discrimination or harassment, or any legal dispute, involve HR or an employment lawyer before writing or sharing. Never submit reviews into an HR system or send them to the employee without the user's explicit approval.

## When to use (and not)

Use for the regular review cycle, mid-year check-ins, promotion cases, and calibration prep. Not for investigations, disciplinary letters, or termination documentation; escalate those.

## What to gather first

- The review template or questions, rating scale, and level/role expectations (career ladder) if they exist.
- The review period.
- Goals or objectives set at the start of the period.
- Evidence: shipped work, metrics, project docs, peer feedback, previous review, 1:1 notes, the person's own notes. Read files the user provides; for engineering roles, git history or merged PRs can supply concrete examples if the user wants that.
- For managers: the person's role and level, and any context (leave, role change, team changes) that affects the period.

## Procedure

### Self-assessment
1. List the period's significant contributions. For each: what the situation was, what you did personally, and the result, with numbers where honest numbers exist.
2. Map contributions to goals and to the expectations of your level. Note which goals changed and why.
3. Include how you worked: collaboration, helping others, decisions made under uncertainty.
4. Name one or two areas to grow, with a specific plan. Self-awareness reads as strength; omitting weaknesses invites the reviewer to name them for you.
5. Keep "I" and "we" honest: claim what you did, credit others for what they did.
6. State what support you need and what you want next (scope, skills, promotion goals).

### Manager review
1. Collect evidence for the **whole** period. Recency bias is the most common distortion; check early-period work deliberately.
2. Assess against the role's expectations, not against the reviewer's personal style or against other individuals.
3. For each strength and each development area, give at least one specific example: situation, behaviour, impact.
4. Describe behaviour and outcomes, not personality ("missed two release deadlines without flagging the risk" rather than "unreliable").
5. Give the rating only after the evidence is written, and check that the words match the rating.
6. Make development actionable: what good looks like, one or two concrete steps, how progress will be checked.
7. Scan for bias: different standards for similar behaviour across people; gendered or coded language ("abrasive", "emotional", "not a culture fit"); penalising approved leave or flexible schedules; halo effect from one big success or failure.
8. Prepare the conversation: the two or three messages that matter most, likely reactions, and questions to invite the person's view.

### Calibration prep
1. For each person: proposed rating, a two-line evidence summary, and the strongest example for and against.
2. Compare like with like (same level and role). Flag cases where ratings and evidence do not match.
3. Look at the distribution across demographic or work-pattern groups if the user has that data and it is appropriate, and investigate gaps rather than explaining them away.
4. Note any rating that would change pay or promotion and what evidence would move it.

## Pitfalls

- Surprises: anything serious in a review should have been raised earlier; if not, acknowledge it.
- Vague praise ("great team player") that gives no information.
- Inventing metrics or examples to fill the template. If evidence is thin, say so and suggest how to gather it.
- Comparing people to each other in the written review.

## Deliverable

Lead with the draft review text, ready to paste into the template. Follow with: the evidence list used, any rating rationale, a bias check note, gaps where evidence was missing, and items that need HR involvement. Remind the user that the review has not been submitted or shared.
